  <section class="mb-10">
    <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-white mb-4 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4">First, a Quick Recap: What Is a SIP?</h2>
    <p class="mb-4">
      A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a method of investing a fixed amount — say ₹5,000 or ₹10,000 — into a mutual fund at regular intervals, typically every month. Think of it as an auto-debit for your future self.
    </p>
    <p class="mb-4">
      A lot of first-time investors actually think SIP itself is an investment product. It isn't. They are a disciplined, automated way to invest in mutual funds. Instead of trying to time the market (which even professionals get wrong more than they would like to admit), SIPs let you buy fund units regularly regardless of market highs and lows. Over time, this averaging effect — often called <strong>Rupee Cost Averaging</strong> — tends to work in your favour.
    </p>
    <p class="mb-4">
      The engine that makes <strong>mutual fund SIP benefits</strong> so powerful over the long run is something you've probably heard of but may have underestimated: <strong>compounding</strong>.
    </p>

    <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-white mb-4 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4">The Power of Compounding in SIP: Why Time Beats Everything Else</h2>
    <p class="mb-4">
      The <strong>power of compounding in SIP</strong> means your returns start earning returns of their own. Early on, the effect is modest — almost invisible. After a few decades, the numbers become hard to believe.

    <p class="mb-4">
      Albert Einstein allegedly called compound interest the "eighth wonder of the world." Whether or not he said it, the math is hard to argue with.
    </p>

    <ul class="list-none space-y-4 mb-4">
      <li class="flex gap-3">
        <span class="text-indigo-500 font-bold mt-1">→</span>
        <div>
          <strong>"I'll wait till I have a higher salary."</strong>
          <p class="mt-1">Many people start SIPs only after hitting a target income. But even ₹500 a month, started early, gets the compounding clock running and builds the habit. You can always increase the amount later.</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li class="flex gap-3">
        <span class="text-indigo-500 font-bold mt-1">→</span>
        <div>
          <strong>"The market is too volatile right now."</strong>
          <p class="mt-1">This is the classic market-timing trap. SIPs are designed specifically to neutralise short-term volatility via rupee cost averaging. In practice, "I'll start when markets settle down" often turns into a 2–3 year delay without people even realizing it and paying the full <strong>cost of waiting to invest</strong>.</p>
        </div>
      </li>
      <li class="flex gap-3">
        <span class="text-indigo-500 font-bold mt-1">→</span>

          <p class="mt-1">Understandable — but you don't need to know every nuance of every fund category before starting. A simple, diversified equity index fund is often enough for beginners to get started.</p>

  <section class="mb-10">
    <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-white mb-2 border-l-4 border-emerald-500 pl-4">A Note on Taxation: What Every Indian SIP Investor Should Know</h2>
    <p class="mb-4">
      SIP returns in equity mutual funds are not tax-free. Understanding the basics helps you plan better and avoid surprises at redemption time.
    </p>
    <ul class="list-disc pl-6 space-y-2 mb-4">
      <li><strong>Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG):</strong> If you redeem equity fund units held for <strong>less than 12 months</strong>, the gains are taxed at <strong>20%</strong> (as per recent budget changes — always verify the current rate).</li>
      <li><strong>Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG):</strong> Gains on equity fund units held for <strong>more than 12 months</strong> are taxed at <strong>12.5%</strong> on gains exceeding ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year.</li>
      <li>Each SIP instalment is treated as a separate purchase, with its own holding period calculated individually at redemption.</li>
      <li>For debt funds, gains are added to your income and taxed at your applicable slab rate, regardless of holding period.</li>
    </ul>
    <p class="text-sm text-gray-500 italic">
      Tax rules change with each Union Budget. Always refer to the latest SEBI and Income Tax Department guidelines, or consult a tax professional for personalised advice.
    </p>
  </section>

  <section class="mb-10">
    <h2 class="text-2xl font-bold text-white mb-5 border-l-4 border-rose-500 pl-4">Common Mistakes to Avoid as a New SIP Investor</h2>
    <div class="space-y-4">
      <div class="border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-900/10 rounded-xl p-5">
        <h3 class="font-semibold text-rose-300 mb-1">❌ Pausing SIPs during market downturns.</h3>
        <p class="text-gray-300 text-sm leading-relaxed">Downturns are actually when SIPs work best — you buy more units at lower prices. Stopping mid-correction is one of the more expensive behavioural errors SIP investors make, and it compounds the original <strong>cost of delaying SIP</strong>.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-900/10 rounded-xl p-5">
        <h3 class="font-semibold text-rose-300 mb-1">❌ Chasing last year's top-performing fund.</h3>
        <p class="text-gray-300 text-sm leading-relaxed">Past returns don't guarantee future performance. Diversifying across fund categories is generally a more prudent approach than rotating into whatever topped last year's rankings.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-900/10 rounded-xl p-5">
        <h3 class="font-semibold text-rose-300 mb-1">❌ Ignoring the expense ratio.</h3>
        <p class="text-gray-300 text-sm leading-relaxed">Even a 0.5% difference in annual expense ratio can shave off lakhs over a 20-year horizon. Many investors prefer direct plans (buying directly from the fund house) over regular plans (bought through a distributor) to keep costs lower.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-900/10 rounded-xl p-5">
        <h3 class="font-semibold text-rose-300 mb-1">❌ Not reviewing the portfolio at all.</h3>
        <p class="text-gray-300 text-sm leading-relaxed">You shouldn't obsess over daily NAV movements, but an annual check to ensure your portfolio still fits your goals is worth doing. Life circumstances change; your allocation should reflect that.</p>
      </div>
      <div class="border border-rose-500/30 bg-rose-900/10 rounded-xl p-5">
        <h3 class="font-semibold text-rose-300 mb-1">❌ Treating SIPs like fixed deposits.</h3>
        <p class="text-gray-300 text-sm leading-relaxed">Equity mutual funds carry market risk. They suit long-term goals where short-term swings are less of a concern — not goals that are a year or two away. Matching the fund type to your goal horizon is non-negotiable.</p>
      </div>
